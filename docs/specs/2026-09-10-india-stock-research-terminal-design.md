# India Stock Research Terminal — Design Spec

**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Status:** Approved, v1 in build
**Framework author:** Nagaraj Balasubramaniam

## 1. Purpose

Turn the sixteen methodology documents in `frameworks/` into working software. The
documents define an institutional research process for NSE and BSE listed companies,
culminating in the V1.2 Standardized Investment Decision Engine: a 100-point score
across seven weighted categories, a set of mandatory override rules, and a fixed
Decision Card output.

The V1.2 engine is authoritative. Where V1.1 and V1.2 disagree, V1.2 wins.

## 2. Split of responsibility

The program owns everything deterministic:

- Weighted score arithmetic and band classification
- Valuation override rules A, B and C
- Thesis-break and expectations overrides
- Margin-of-safety classification
- Expected value when scenario probabilities are supplied
- The final action matrix
- Report rendering, including the mandatory section 32 attribution and disclaimer

The analyst owns the seven raw 1–10 judgement scores. The program proposes a draft for
each, shows the computed evidence behind the proposal, and labels it a model
suggestion. Every draft is overridable and an override never needs justification to the
program, only a note for the record.

The program never invents a number. A missing input is reported as missing and blocks
the affected score until supplied or explicitly waived.

## 3. Two halves, one repository

### 3.1 Private terminal

Runs on the analyst's machine, bound to `127.0.0.1`. Python with FastAPI, SQLite for
storage, and a hand-written interface in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

*Changed during the build.* The plan called for React with Vite and Tailwind. The actual
interaction surface turned out small: seven sliders, some text fields, ten checkboxes and a
publish button. A build step and a dependency tree were not worth it for a local, zero-cost
tool, and removing them means the project runs with Python alone.

It fetches free data, computes the tables, proposes scores, runs the engine and renders
reports. No authentication, because it is not reachable off the machine.

### 3.2 Public site

Static HTML generated by the publish step and served by GitHub Pages from the `site/`
directory via a GitHub Actions workflow. Read-only. No login, no accounts, no
server-side code, no analytics.

Published reports include the final action verdict, framed as an educational
demonstration of the scoring framework. Every page carries the section 32 attribution
and the SEBI disclaimer.

### 3.3 Boundary

Publishing is always an explicit analyst action. The following never enter version
control:

- `terminal.db`, the local SQLite database
- `frameworks/`, the source methodology documents, which remain private intellectual
  property
- Any draft or unpublished report

The repository is public because free GitHub Pages requires it. The `.gitignore` is
therefore a privacy control, not housekeeping, and is written before the first commit.

## 4. Data sources, all free

| Source | Provides | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `yfinance` | Annual and quarterly statements, price history, ratios, sector | No key. Scrapes Yahoo, can break. |
| NSE public JSON | Live quote, delivery volumes, corporate actions, shareholding | No key. Needs cookie priming and polite headers. Rate limited. |
| Manual entry | Anything the above miss, plus consensus and guidance | Always available as a fallback. |

Every stored figure carries `value`, `source`, `as_of` and `basis`, where basis
distinguishes consolidated from standalone. The report prints source and date for every
material number, as the master methodology requires.

Free reliable consensus estimates for Indian equities do not exist. The expectations
section is manual entry in v1 rather than fabricated.

## 5. Module design

```
app/
  providers/     yahoo.py, nse.py         → SourcedValue records
  core/
    metrics.py    five-year and eight-quarter tables, CAGRs, derived ratios
    forensics.py  red-flag score per framework 04
    valuation.py  relative multiples, DCF, WACC, scenarios, margin of safety
    technical.py  DMA, RSI, MACD, Bollinger, ATR, relative strength → score /10
    drafts.py     heuristic raw-score proposals with evidence
    scoring.py    V1.2 engine: weights, bands, overrides, action matrix, audit
    sectors.py    sector adapters
  report/         render.py, publish.py
  api/            routes.py
web/              workbench interface, no build step
site/             generated public output
tests/            pytest
```

`core/scoring.py` is pure. It takes raw scores and context, returns a decision. No
input or output, no database, no network. It is developed test-first and its rules map
one-to-one onto numbered sections of framework 14.

## 6. The engine, precisely

### 6.1 Weights

| Category | Weight |
|---|---|
| Quality | 20 |
| Growth | 20 |
| Earnings | 15 |
| Valuation | 20 |
| Moat | 10 |
| Catalysts | 5 |
| Risk | 10 |

Weighted contribution is `raw / 10 × weight`. Risk is scored in reverse, 10 being low
risk. Technical analysis is excluded from the 100 points and reported separately.

### 6.2 Bands

85–100 HIGH CONVICTION, 75–84 ATTRACTIVE, 65–74 WATCHLIST, 50–64 CAUTION, below 50
AVOID. Bands are starting classifications that overrides may change.

### 6.3 Overrides

- **Rule A**: quality ≥ 8, growth ≥ 8, moat ≥ 8 and valuation ≤ 3 blocks BUY and labels
  the report "high-quality business, low margin of safety".
- **Rule B**: valuation ≥ 8 with quality ≤ 4, earnings ≤ 4 and risk ≤ 4 blocks any
  attractive classification and labels it "cheap but fundamentally weak".
- **Rule C**: valuation flagged extreme requires a written multiple-compression note
  before the report can be published.
- **Thesis break**: any flagged structural break restricts the action to REDUCE or EXIT.
- **Expectations**: the gap between expected and likely growth is classified and shown.

### 6.4 Margin of safety

The framework is qualitative here, so the thresholds are an explicit model assumption
and are shown as such: discount to base value of 25% or more is HIGH, 10% to 25% is
MODERATE, within 10% either way is LOW, and a price more than 10% above base value is
NEGATIVE. Configurable per report.

### 6.5 Action matrix

BUY normally requires 85 or above; ACCUMULATE 75; HOLD 65. A BUY below 85 is permitted
only with a written asymmetry justification, which the report prints. REDUCE, EXIT and
WATCHLIST follow section 19 of framework 14.

### 6.6 Publication gate

*Changed after the build.* The spec originally required all ten section 21 audit
questions to be answered before publishing. At the analyst's direction the audit was
removed entirely, so publication is now blocked only where the engine cannot stand
behind the page: an unclassified margin of safety, or an extreme valuation flagged
without the rule C multiple-compression note.

## 7. Technical score

Computed from price history, never manual. Transparent additive rubric over trend
against the 20, 50, 100 and 200 day moving averages, RSI band, MACD signal, volume
confirmation, relative strength against the Nifty, and position within the 52-week
range. The rubric is printed with the score so the number is never a black box.

## 8. Sector adapters

v1 ships two. The generic adapter covers most sectors. The banking and NBFC adapter
suppresses enterprise-value multiples, drives valuation from price to book against
sustainable return on equity, and swaps the evidence set to credit growth, net interest
margin, gross and net non-performing assets, slippages, credit cost, provision
coverage, capital adequacy and CASA. Further sector panels follow in v2.

## 9. Interface

A dark terminal aesthetic, modern rather than retro. Near-black canvas, one restrained
accent, a serious text face paired with a monospace so figure columns align. Dense
tables, sparklines in the five-year rows, one animated dial for the score, semantic
colour for verdict and thesis status.

Private workspace tabs: Overview, Financials, Forensics, Valuation, Technical, Scoring,
Decision. Public pages are pre-rendered and readable on a phone.

## 10. Out of scope for v1

Portfolio overlay, thesis tracker and monitoring dashboard, catalyst calendar, macro
regime dashboard, deeper per-sector panels, quarterly update workflow.

## 11. Known risks

- `yfinance` quarterly cash-flow coverage for Indian tickers may be incomplete. Manual
  entry fills gaps and the report flags them.
- NSE endpoints may throttle or block. All NSE data is optional; nothing depends on it.
- Draft scores are heuristics, not truth. They are labelled as suggestions everywhere
  they appear.
- Publishing equity analysis with an action verdict is a decision the analyst has taken
  knowingly. The educational framing and disclaimer appear on every page.
